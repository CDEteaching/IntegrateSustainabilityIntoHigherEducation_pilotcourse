Session 04: Presentation of Action Plans

Session 04 Live · approx. 2h (async option available) Presentations & discussion

Presentation of Action Plans

The closing session brings the course’s individual work together: participants present their completed ESD Action Plans, identify opportunities for collaboration, and reflect on and evaluate the course as a whole.

Session Overview

Format Recommended as a live synchronous session (on-site or video call), since it centers on presentations and live group discussion. If a live session is not feasible: run as a time-boxed asynchronous window (e.g. one week) with recorded action-plan presentations and written peer feedback in place of live presentation and discussion.
Duration Approx. 2h live (scales with group size, given 6-minute presentations per participant/group). Asynchronous equivalent: an open review window of several days, with roughly 60-90 minutes of individual engagement time (viewing others’ presentations, giving feedback, completing the evaluation).
Preparation for participants A completed ESD Action Plan (Tasks after Sessions 1-3), and a prepared short presentation of it (approx. 6 minutes)

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Deepen their understanding of ESD and constructive alignment.
  2. Present their own action plan.
  3. Identify collaboration opportunities with other lecturers, departments, or external organizations working on sustainability initiatives.

Session Outline

1. Muddiest point (variable, approx. 10 min)
Addressing points of confusion raised after Session 3.
2. Presentation of Action Plans (variable, 6 min per participant)
Current state, opportunities identified, developed session (learning outcomes, activities, assessment); listeners take notes and prepare feedback.
3. Synthesis (10 min)
Key insights across the presented action plans (challenges, need for further support, etc.).

4. Group discussion: collaboration opportunities (10 min)

5. Harvesting of the group discussion (10 min)
Highlights, synergies, commonalities.

6. Open Q&A (10 min)

7. Main learnings from the course and course evaluation (variable, approx. 10 min)

Materials

Required reading

  • None separately assigned. Sources cited on the slides serve as background/citation material rather than mandatory pre-reading.

Slides

  • Session 04 slides (PowerPoint, 19 slides)
  • Session 04 Reader (background reading) · Reader as PDF

Optional / going deeper

  • Participants’ own completed ESD Action Plans (Tasks after Sessions 1-3), the core “material” of this session.

Preparation tasks & learning activities

Before the session

Finalize the ESD Action Plan (Tasks after Sessions 1-3) and prepare a short (approx. 6-minute) presentation of it.

During the session

  • Present (or submit a recorded presentation of) your action plan.
  • Take notes and prepare constructive feedback on other participants’ presentations.
  • Contribute to the group discussion on collaboration opportunities.
  • Complete the course evaluation survey.

After the session

No formal task is defined in the course materials after this closing session. Consider suggesting, as an optional next step, that participants share their action plan with their department or apply it in an upcoming course.

Recommendation for facilitation

  • Use a shared virtual board (e.g. Miro) for noting down the collaboration-opportunities discussion, and a live polling tool (e.g. Menti) for the “main learnings” question.
  • In a live session, the “main learnings of the course” question works well as an open plenary discussion following the live poll, giving participants a chance to hear and build on each other’s takeaways, not just see them aggregated. In a self-paced/asynchronous format, replace this with an individual written reflection using the same prompt.
  • Since action plans span many disciplines, structure peer feedback prompts to ask specifically how a presented idea could translate to a different field, not just whether it works well within its own discipline; this is where the cross-disciplinary value of the session lies.
  • Where a fully live session with all participants is not feasible, keep the peer-feedback element by running a time-boxed asynchronous window with recorded presentations and written comments, rather than dropping it.
  • Closing evaluation: the course should close with an evaluation, adapted to reflect the actual format in which it was delivered, the content covered, the delivery mode (live vs. self-paced vs. hybrid), and, where relevant, the specific adaptations made for that cohort’s context.
